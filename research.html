Research

Science that drives smarter wild pig management

One of the Task Force’s core objectives is to identify knowledge gaps in the biology, ecology, and management of wild pigs, and to promote and facilitate coordinated research and international collaboration that fills them.

The NWPTF Research Subcommittee brings together scientists from universities, agencies, and the private sector to set shared priorities, share findings, and translate science into practice.

Research priorities

Where the science is focused

Wild pig research spans many disciplines. These are among the areas most critical to effective, science-based management.

Population & ecology

Reproduction, movement, density, and habitat use: the dynamics that determine how fast populations grow and spread.

Control methods

Evaluating the effectiveness of trapping, toxicants, and other tools, including whole-sounder removal and emerging techniques.

Disease surveillance

Monitoring diseases carried by wild pigs and the risks they pose to livestock, wildlife, and human health.

Damage & economics

Quantifying impacts to agriculture, natural resources, and infrastructure to inform funding and policy.

Monitoring & detection

Trail cameras, surveys, and modeling that map where populations are establishing and how their range is changing.

Human dimensions

Understanding attitudes, hunting culture, and behavior that shape how management is received and adopted.
