About

Coordinating a continent-wide response to invasive wild pigs

The National Wild Pig Task Force is a coordinating body that brings together the researchers, natural resource professionals, agencies, and partners working to reduce the damage caused by invasive wild pigs across North America.

Wild pigs are one of the most destructive invasive species on the continent. They damage agriculture, degrade natural habitats and water quality, prey on native wildlife, and carry diseases that threaten livestock and human health. The problem crosses property lines, county lines, and state lines, which means no single landowner or agency can solve it alone.

What we do

The Task Force serves as a shared platform for coordination and knowledge exchange. Our work centers on connecting the people and institutions confronting wild pig impacts so that effort is not duplicated and hard-won lessons are shared widely.

  • Convening researchers, agency staff, and practitioners around common challenges
  • Curating and sharing science, data, and field-tested management guidance
  • Supporting communication and outreach that reaches the people who need it
  • Providing a national point of contact for an issue that spans jurisdictions

How we work

The Task Force operates through committees and volunteer members drawn from federal and state agencies, universities, and the private sector. This mix is intentional. Effective wild pig management depends on the people doing research, the people setting policy, and the people in the field all working from a shared understanding.
